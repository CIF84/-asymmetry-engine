# Blind Evidence Capsule — AE Economic Mechanism Generation

## Purpose
This capsule is the complete evidence input for one independent reasoning task:

> Generate the abstract economic mechanism space by which an information/reasoning system with the demonstrated characteristics below could create recurring, increasingly low-maintenance captured value.

This capsule contains descriptive project facts and objective constraints only. It intentionally contains no prior strategy recommendations, named candidate business models, or prior mechanism taxonomy.

Do not use any other repository material or public web source for this task.

## Economic objective
The system's long-run objective is to produce a portfolio of recurring economic value with progressively lower mechanical human effort, greater autonomy, reusable learning/infrastructure, and acceptable risk.

Commercial success is not yet established.

First economic proof, repeatability, low maintenance, and portfolio replication are distinct milestones.

## Demonstrated capability boundary
Repository evidence before the current strategy work supports these bounded capabilities:

- collecting and normalizing information from some legitimate public or licensed sources;
- preserving source identity, timestamps, revisions and provenance;
- turning heterogeneous evidence into falsifiable opportunity hypotheses through substantial semantic judgment;
- rejecting hypotheses when a necessary condition fails;
- testing whether an adequate existing resolution already removes a proposed information gap;
- recovering and composing information into bounded decision-ready resolutions in some cases;
- explicitly preserving unsupported claims and UNKNOWN states;
- reasoning about decision actors, beneficiaries and economic consequence without assuming they are buyers or payers;
- executing bounded repository-centered research cheaply;
- using human authorization for consequential external action;
- maintaining auditable experiment history and operator-visible research state.

The system has not demonstrated generic autonomous opportunity detection, generic automated decision resolution, autonomous economic action, scalable distribution, or portfolio operation.

## Current economic frontier
No durable evidence currently establishes:

- economic effect caused by the system's resolution;
- valid willingness-to-pay or equivalent exchange evidence;
- a completed attributable transaction;
- repeatable economic exchange;
- repeatable captured value;
- scalable unit economics;
- a low-maintenance recurring economic mechanism.

One historical attempt explicitly intended to test a paid offer but failed before valid market exposure. Therefore it supplied no valid demand or willingness-to-pay conclusion.

Some later work reached useful decision-ready resolutions or bounded decision-state refinement, but downstream implementation and economic outcomes remained unobserved.

## Historical commercial-distance patterns
Across selected historical chains:

- useful information/resolutions could sometimes be produced cheaply;
- identifying a decision actor did not establish an economic buyer or payer;
- public delivery did not necessarily establish actor exposure;
- actor exposure or response was sometimes difficult or impossible to observe;
- platform/authentication/moderation constraints sometimes blocked or obscured interaction;
- an exact adequate existing solution could invalidate a proposed residual information gap;
- inaccessible actor surfaces could stop otherwise useful resolution work;
- economic consequence in the underlying decision did not establish economic value attributable to the system;
- no historical chain established repeatable value capture.

These are historical observations, not claims that future economic mechanisms must involve actors, customers, content, products, or any particular transaction form.

## Experiment-economics observations
Historical work supports:

- cheap rejection can save unnecessary deeper work;
- external waiting can lengthen individual experiments;
- some useful work can overlap waiting;
- historical evidence does not establish that scheduling/parallelism was the main economic bottleneck;
- external cash spend was usually very low, while human and compute costs were incompletely measured;
- low external spend is not evidence of low total economic cost;
- building more infrastructure has not yet established economic value.

## Operator / portfolio constraints
Use these only as evaluation constraints after generating the mechanism space:

- recurring value is preferred to isolated one-off value;
- progressively lower ongoing human effort is preferred;
- automation and reusable mechanics are desirable when they reduce recurring burden;
- autonomy is valuable;
- concentration and platform dependency are costs;
- capital requirements and downside matter;
- operational complexity, customer support and maintenance are costs;
- bounded initial manual work is acceptable if it produces strong learning;
- no existing project component is entitled to future investment because of sunk cost;
- economic outcomes ultimately outrank internal elegance.

Do not use these constraints to suppress candidate generation prematurely.

## Epistemic constraints
Preserve these distinctions:

- signal is not demand;
- economic consequence is not captured value;
- delivery is not exposure;
- exposure is not effect;
- effect is not transaction;
- transaction is not repeatability;
- repeatability is not necessarily low maintenance;
- unknown is not negative evidence;
- historical absence of a tested mechanism is not proof that mechanism cannot work.

## Blind task

### A. Generate abstract economic mechanism families
Independently derive the high-level ways the described system could create and capture economic value.

Start from first principles:
- what economic event creates payoff;
- who or what transfers value;
- what the system owns or controls;
- whether another party must buy directly from the system;
- whether the system performs an economic action itself;
- what repeats;
- what capital/operational burden exists;
- what could become low-maintenance.

Do not begin from a memorized list of business models.

For each abstract family provide:
- definition;
- payoff event;
- value-transfer path;
- recurrence mechanism;
- principal capital requirement;
- principal operational burden;
- principal dependency;
- principal failure mode;
- plausible path toward low maintenance.

Only after defining an abstract family may you give familiar real-world implementations as illustrative examples.

### B. Completeness / missing-opposite challenge
Challenge the generated space from first principles.

Ask whether the map covers materially different cases involving:
- value transferred by another party versus value realized through the system's own position/action;
- owning versus not owning fulfillment;
- requiring versus not requiring a direct customer;
- recurring events versus durable assets;
- intermediation versus principal risk-taking;
- monetizing information, access, attention, risk-bearing, ownership, coordination, intellectual property, or other economically distinct sources;
- transferring commercialization to another party;
- mechanisms that do not fit the initial dimensions.

For each favored family, explicitly seek its conceptual opposite.

Merge families that differ only by implementation rather than economic topology. Split families when payoff/risk/recurrence economics are materially different.

### C. Structural objective-fit screen
Without current-market research, assess structural compatibility with:
- recurrence;
- low ongoing human burden;
- automation;
- bounded initial validation;
- portfolio replication;
- acceptable capital/downside;
- resilience to concentration/platform dependency.

Do not select a winner. This is not a feasibility tournament.
