# Opportunity Model — Evidence Through Experiment 035

## Status

ATLAS consolidation checkpoint.

This document reconstructs the current opportunity model from evidence accumulated through Experiments 001–035. It is a **provisional learned model**, not a product architecture, scoring formula, database schema, or automation specification.

Experiments 030 and 035 are still awaiting final interaction outcomes. Their unresolved evidence must challenge this model later rather than being silently incorporated now.

---

## 1. Why this document exists

The original Asymmetry Engine hypothesis was approximately:

```text
PUBLIC DATA → SIGNAL → ASYMMETRY → OPPORTUNITY → PRODUCT → REVENUE
```

Repeated experiments showed that this compresses several distinct questions into the word `opportunity`.

The Engine now needs three separate models:

1. **Opportunity anatomy** — what must be true for an opportunity to exist?
2. **Opportunity lifecycle** — in what order should those truths be learned cheaply?
3. **Research policy** — given current uncertainty, what observation or experiment should happen next?

Confusing these creates premature scoring, unnecessary research, inaccessible experiments, and products built before economic evidence exists.

# Part I — Opportunity anatomy

## 2. Current economic object

The strongest evidence-supported object is no longer a generic information asymmetry.

It is:

> **An economically consequential decision under uncertainty where relevant information is recoverable, existing resolution is inadequate, a better resolution can be constructed, the decision actor can legitimately be reached, and the effect of the resolution can be observed.**

A commercial opportunity additionally requires evidence that the resulting value can eventually be captured and repeated.

Current conceptual structure:

```text
ACTOR
  ↓
LIVE DECISION
  ↓
ECONOMIC CONSEQUENCE
  ↓
MEANINGFUL UNCERTAINTY
  ↓
RECOVERABLE INFORMATION
  ↓
INADEQUATE EXISTING RESOLUTION
  ↓
RESOLUTION FEASIBILITY
  ↓
LEGITIMATE ACTOR / DECISION ACCESS
  ↓
OBSERVABLE RESOLUTION EFFECT
  ↓
VALUE CREATED ?
  ↓
VALUE CAPTURE ?
  ↓
REPEATABILITY ?
```

The first eight elements are increasingly evidence-backed. The last three remain materially under-tested.

---

## 3. Anatomy components and evidence provenance

### A. Actor

**Question:** Is there a specific actor facing the decision?

**Why it exists:** Generic populations hide whether a real decision context can be observed or influenced.

**Evidence:** Experiments 026–028 and 031–032.

**Failure prevented:** Constructing resolutions for hypothetical users or actors who cannot be reached.

**Current maturity:** REPLICATED PRINCIPLE.

---

### B. Live decision

**Question:** Is the actor currently deciding something rather than merely discussing a topic or reporting past friction?

**Why it exists:** Friction, dissatisfaction, curiosity, and historical complaints are not equivalent to pre-commitment decision behavior.

**Evidence:** Experiments 017–020, 028, 031–032.

**Failure prevented:** Mistaking behavioral evidence for actionable demand.

**Current maturity:** REPLICATED PRINCIPLE.

---

### C. Economic consequence

**Question:** Does the decision have a meaningful cost, risk, time, performance, revenue, or other economic consequence?

**Why it exists:** Information gaps without consequence may be interesting but have weak value-creation potential.

**Evidence:** Experiments 013–020 and 023–025.

**Failure prevented:** Treating information novelty or dispersion as opportunity value.

**Current maturity:** REPLICATED PRINCIPLE.

---

### D. Meaningful uncertainty

**Question:** Is something decision-relevant genuinely unresolved for the actor?

**Why it exists:** A decision with obvious dominant action does not require resolution.

**Evidence:** Experiments 017–020, 024–025, 028–029, 033–034.

**Failure prevented:** Solving questions whose answers are already operationally obvious.

**Current maturity:** REPLICATED PRINCIPLE.

---

### E. Recoverable information

**Question:** Can the uncertainty be reduced using information legitimately obtainable before commitment?

**Why it exists:** Some important uncertainty depends on private state, physical inspection, future outcomes, inaccessible data, or facts that cannot be established cheaply enough.

**Evidence:** Experiments 017–019, 024–025, 031–033.

**Failure prevented:** Building resolution mechanisms around unknowable or prohibitively expensive inputs.

**Current maturity:** REPLICATED PRINCIPLE.

---

### F. Inadequate existing resolution

**Question:** Does the exact decision remain insufficiently resolved by existing tools, services, workflows, incumbents, or free information?

Functional comparison should use:

```text
ACTOR × DECISION × INPUTS × RESOLUTION × OUTPUT × TIMING
```

**Why it exists:** Information can be hard to find while the actual decision is already solved adequately elsewhere.

**Evidence:** Experiment 020 was the decisive failure; 021–024, 031–033 repeatedly reinforced the gate.

**Failure prevented:** Rebuilding solved problems and confusing originality with residual opportunity.

**Current maturity:** REPLICATED PRINCIPLE.

---

### G. Resolution feasibility

**Question:** Can recoverable evidence actually be transformed into a defensible decision resolution?

**Why it exists:** Available information does not automatically compose into useful advice.

**Evidence:** Experiments 024–025, 029, 033–034.

**Failure prevented:** Assuming aggregation equals resolution.

**Current maturity:** REPLICATED PRINCIPLE, with domain-general scope still provisional.

---

### H. Legitimate actor / decision access

**Question:** Can the resolution legitimately reach the relevant actor at the relevant decision surface?

This decomposes into:

```text
SURFACE ACCESS
≠ ACTOR ACCESS
≠ INTERVENTION PERMISSION
≠ ACTOR EXPOSURE
```

**Why it exists:** A technically public or communicable surface may still be inaccessible, gatekept, prohibited, or incapable of reaching the actual decision participant.

**Evidence:** Experiments 014, 026–028, 031–032 and the execution controls in 030/035.

**Failure prevented:** Producing resolutions that cannot enter real decision contexts or require illegitimate interaction.

**Current maturity:** REPLICATED PRINCIPLE.

---

### I. Observable resolution effect

**Question:** If the resolution reaches the actor, can a meaningful effect on understanding, framing, shortlist, confidence, next action, or decision state be observed?

**Why it exists:** Communication accessibility alone does not make an experiment interpretable.

**Evidence:** Experiments 026–032 established the need. Experiments 030 and 035 are the first direct mechanism tests and remain unresolved at this checkpoint.

**Failure prevented:** Running interactions where no response cannot distinguish delivery, exposure, comprehension, trust, or value.

**Current maturity:** PROVISIONAL PRINCIPLE pending 030/035.

---

### J. Value creation

**Question:** Does better resolution actually improve the actor's decision or reduce economically meaningful uncertainty?

**Why it exists:** A correct or appreciated answer is not necessarily valuable.

**Evidence:** Experiments 025, 029, and 034 show decision-space compression is technically possible; 030 and 035 test whether it affects actors.

**Failure prevented:** Equating information quality with economic value.

**Current maturity:** HYPOTHESIS.

---

### K. Value capture

**Question:** Can some of created value be captured through payment or another economically useful exchange?

**Why it exists:** Value creation does not imply a business model.

**Evidence:** Experiment 014 attempted a paid pilot but never achieved valid market exposure. No later experiment establishes willingness to pay.

**Failure prevented:** Calling useful mechanisms businesses before transaction evidence.

**Current maturity:** HYPOTHESIS / UNTESTED.

---

### L. Repeatability

**Question:** Does the mechanism recur across actors/decisions often enough to support a repeatable product or portfolio asset?

**Why it exists:** A valuable one-off resolution may still be consulting, research, or luck rather than a scalable opportunity.

**Evidence:** Not established.

**Failure prevented:** Generalizing from n=1 mechanism evidence to market opportunity.

**Current maturity:** UNTESTED.

# Part II — Opportunity lifecycle

## 4. Anatomy is not search order

The components above describe what may ultimately need to be true. They do **not** imply that research should test them in that order.

RADAR's practical objective is closer to:

> **Find the cheapest decision-relevant observation that can destroy the hypothesis while preserving credible survivors.**

Research order should therefore depend on expected information value, cost, reversibility, experimentability, and control feasibility.

---

## 5. Current evidence-derived lifecycle

Experiments 020–035 suggest the following provisional search sequence:

```text
OBSERVE SIGNAL
      ↓
IDENTIFY ACTOR + LIVE DECISION
      ↓
CHECK INTERVENTION TOPOLOGY
 actor access + legitimate path + observable effect
      ↓
CHECK ECONOMIC CONSEQUENCE
      ↓
IDENTIFY DECISION-RELEVANT UNCERTAINTY
      ↓
CHECK RECOVERABILITY
      ↓
CHECK EXACT EXISTING RESOLUTION
      ↓
RUN CHEAPEST BOUNDED DISCRIMINATOR
      ↓
CONSTRUCT DISPOSABLE RESOLUTION
      ↓
ADVERSARIALLY CHALLENGE IT
      ↓
AUTHORIZED REAL-WORLD INTERACTION
      ↓
MEASURE DECISION EFFECT
      ↓
TEST VALUE / CAPTURE / REPEATABILITY ?
```

This ordering is provisional. It is not a rigid pipeline.

The main learned change is that **experimentability should often be checked much earlier than resolution construction**. Specs 026–032 repeatedly showed that strong informational opportunities can become economically useless experiments if the actor or effect cannot be observed.

---

## 6. Hard gates versus heuristics

### Strong current kill conditions

These have repeatedly destroyed candidates:

- no identifiable/live decision;
- weak or absent economic consequence;
- decision-relevant information not recoverable at acceptable cost;
- exact existing resolver already solves the job adequately;
- actor cannot legitimately be reached;
- effect cannot be observed well enough for the intended experiment;
- controls/regulation make the experiment illegitimate or uneconomic.

They behave more like constraints than additive score components.

### Conditional gates

These depend on experiment design:

- private state may survive if it can be bounded and legitimately elicited;
- separate intervention surfaces may survive if exposure/effect remain strong;
- incomplete resolution may survive if one cheap discriminator can resolve the dominant uncertainty;
- architectural uncertainty may survive when reversibility is cheap and waiting has no evidence-supported payoff.

### Heuristics, not gates

Current evidence does **not** justify treating these as universal requirements:

- Reddit/forum presence;
- high signal frequency;
- novelty;
- broad market size;
- automation potential;
- software deliverability;
- quantitative opportunity score;
- a generic percentage survival threshold.

# Part III — Research policy

## 7. Research policy is a scheduler over uncertainty

The Engine should not ask merely:

> What gate comes next?

It should ask:

> **What observation now has the highest expected ability to change the decision to continue, stop, resolve, interact, or escalate?**

Conceptually:

```text
CURRENT BELIEF STATE
      ↓
UNRESOLVED UNCERTAINTIES
      ↓
POSSIBLE OBSERVATIONS / EXPERIMENTS
      ↓
compare:
  decision relevance
  falsification power
  information potential
  cost / time
  reversibility
  experimentability
  control feasibility
      ↓
INDEPENDENT CHALLENGE
      ↓
NEXT ACTION
```

Do not implement a numeric scheduler yet. The evidence supports the decision logic, not calibrated weights.

---

## 8. Research-policy changes learned empirically

The discovery policy has changed because experiments changed the bottleneck:

```text
ASYMMETRY-FIRST
      ↓
BEHAVIOR-FIRST
      ↓
SIGNAL-NATIVE
      ↓
PRE-CONSOLIDATION
      ↓
ACCESSIBLE-SURFACE-FIRST
      ↓
ACTOR + EFFECT OBSERVABILITY FIRST
```

This is not taxonomy churn. Each transition responded to an observed failure mode.

The most recent sequence is particularly informative:

```text
031
candidate generation abundant
but actor access / effect observability weak
      ↓
032
actor + intervention + effect topology selected deliberately
      ↓
033
bottleneck moved to residual resolution gap
      ↓
034
FORGE produced challenged decision-ready resolution
      ↓
035
real interaction now waiting on reality
```

A bottleneck moving later in the lifecycle is evidence of research-policy improvement even before commercial success.

# Part IV — Resolution as value mechanism

## 9. Decision compression

Experiments 025, 029, 033, and 034 suggest a recurring transformation:

```text
UNSTRUCTURED UNCERTAINTY
      ↓
STRUCTURED UNCERTAINTY
      ↓
EXPLICIT OPTIONS
      ↓
DOMINANT DISCRIMINATORS
      ↓
TESTABLE NEXT QUESTION
      ↓
DECISION-READY RESOLUTION
```

A resolution need not provide categorical certainty.

It may create value by:

- eliminating dominated options;
- separating facts from assumptions;
- exposing missing decision-sensitive information;
- identifying the cheapest next test;
- bounding future uncertainty;
- making reversal evidence explicit.

This is currently the strongest candidate for a reusable FORGE capability.

Whether actors experience that compression as valuable remains under direct test.

---

## 10. Disposable before durable

The evidence supports:

> **Use the cheapest representation capable of producing the next economic evidence.**

A spreadsheet, brief, calculation, comment, manual join, or one-off analysis can be superior to software when the unresolved question is behavioral or economic.

Software should follow demonstrated repeated need, not substitute for missing evidence.

# Part VI — What the model rejects

## 12. Rejected simplifications

Evidence through 035 rejects or materially weakens these formulations:

### `friction = demand`

False. Friction may never become pre-decision behavior or economic action.

### `price dispersion = information asymmetry`

False. Dispersion can reflect legitimate heterogeneity and may already be resolved adequately.

### `public information + transformation = opportunity`

Insufficient. The actor, decision, residual resolution gap, experimentability, and effect matter.

### `better answer = value`

Unproven. Actor decision effect is required.

### `value = business`

False. Capture and repeatability remain separate evidence layers.

### `accessible surface = experimentable opportunity`

False. Actor access, intervention permission, exposure, and effect observability are distinct.

### `more candidates = better RADAR`

False. Candidate quality includes how cheaply hypotheses can be discriminated and brought into observable real decisions.

### `automation = progress`

False. Automation multiplies validated mechanisms; it does not repair weak economics.

# Part VII — Open questions

## 13. Questions reality has not answered yet

The largest unresolved relationships are:

1. Does decision compression reliably create observable actor value?
2. Does selecting actor + effect topology early materially improve interaction evidence yield?
3. What evidence distinguishes decision effect from politeness, engagement, or intellectual interest?
4. When does decision effect become willingness to pay?
5. Which resolution mechanisms repeat across domains rather than remain bespoke analysis?
6. How often does a residual exact-resolution gap coexist with reachable economic demand?
7. What is the false-negative cost of the current aggressive falsification policy?
8. Which gates are genuinely universal versus artifacts of the opportunity families tested so far?
9. When should a repeated manual resolution become implemented capability?
10. What evidence shows that the Engine is compounding economically rather than merely becoming better at rejection?

Experiments 030 and 035 directly inform questions 1–3. They should be evaluated against this frozen checkpoint after their observation windows close.

# Part IX — Relationship to the Engine

## 15. Current system interpretation

The Engine can now be described more precisely as:

> **An experimental system for discovering economically consequential decisions under resolvable uncertainty, cheaply testing whether better information improves those decisions, and learning which resolution mechanisms can create and eventually capture repeatable value.**

Its loops are increasingly distinct:

```text
ATLAS
accumulated learned state
      ↑                         ↓
LEARN ← MEASURE ← INTERACT ← FORGE ← RADAR ← OBSERVE
```

RADAR is not primarily a candidate-ranking engine. It is a sequential falsification and uncertainty-reduction process.

FORGE is not primarily a product builder. It is a resolution-construction and epistemic instrument.

INTERACT is not outreach. It is controlled exposure of a resolution to a real decision context.

ATLAS is not documentation storage. It is the accumulated state that should improve future research policy.

---

## 16. Compounding criterion

The Engine begins to compound when accumulated learning causes future economic evidence to arrive faster, cheaper, or with higher discriminating power.

Observable signs include:

- earlier rejection of structurally weak candidates;
- fewer repeated failure modes;
- movement of bottlenecks later in the opportunity lifecycle;
- better selection of experimentally reachable decisions;
- cheaper discriminators;
- more defensible disposable resolutions;
- higher-yield interactions;
- reusable resolution patterns;
- better prediction of which candidates survive;
- decreasing human/mechanical effort per unit of economic evidence.

Candidate count alone is not compounding.

# Part X — Freeze and challenge protocol

## 17. What happens next

This checkpoint is intentionally frozen while Experiments 030 and 035 await reality.

When those experiments resolve:

1. do not rewrite this model immediately;
2. classify each result against the relevant claims above;
3. identify which principles are SUPPORTED, WEAKENED, CONTRADICTED, or STILL UNTESTED;
4. distinguish failure of a candidate from failure of the model;
5. change the model only where the new evidence has discriminating power;
6. preserve superseded reasoning in Git history.

The next useful formalization should be evidence-triggered, not calendar-triggered.

---

## 18. Governing principle

> **An opportunity model should explain why hypotheses survive reality, not make every new hypothesis look like an opportunity.**
