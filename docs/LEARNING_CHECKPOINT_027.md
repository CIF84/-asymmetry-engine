# Learning Checkpoint 027 — Distribution Feasibility as an Empirical Gate

## Outcome

Spec 027 returned **C — DISTRIBUTION NOT FEASIBLE ENOUGH / PARK** for the bounded Canadian September 8 counter-tariff opportunity.

The opportunity is parked despite a demonstrated asymmetry, material economic consequence, a proven live resolution gap, and a correct disposable resolution.

This is a successful Engine outcome because the branch was rejected on an empirically observed economic constraint before durable product investment.

## What Spec 027 established

Relevant Canadian SME importers are present on several real surfaces, especially broker/freight ecosystems, association channels, exact-intent search, and authenticated customs workflows.

However, no investigated path combined all of the following at acceptable effort:

- relevant actor presence;
- live pre-decision timing;
- decision-linked intent;
- independent project reachability;
- repeatable experimental throughput;
- compatibility with the bounded Spec 025 resolution.

The strongest operational paths were broker/freight and CFIB-style association channels. Both depend on gatekeeper permission, institutional trust, and segmentation that the project does not currently possess.

Organic search could theoretically become repeatable, but only after building and publishing a trusted intake surface and acquiring traffic. Public communities were too sparse and irregular. Government hubs had no accessible case-resolution interface. CARM was highly relevant but authenticated and integration-dependent.

The opportunity is not being parked because the problem is fake or the resolution is weak. It is being parked because the relevant decision context is not sufficiently accessible to the project.

## Experimentability refinement

The project previously treated experimentability mainly as:

> Can we cheaply obtain enough observations to reduce uncertainty about an opportunity?

Spec 027 shows that this must be decomposed into two distinct properties.

### Discovery experimentability

Can RADAR cheaply observe enough evidence to identify, reconstruct, and reject or advance an opportunity?

### Intervention experimentability

Can FORGE cheaply place a resolution into enough real decisions to observe its effect?

The tariff opportunity scored well on discovery experimentability and poorly on intervention experimentability.

This distinction should remain explicit.

## Accessible decision surface

Spec 027 suggests a stronger concept than generic distribution:

> **Accessible decision surface** — a surface where the affected actor becomes observable or reachable while the economically consequential decision is still live, and where a bounded resolution can be introduced with tolerable friction.

A large theoretical market can have a poor accessible decision surface if actors are hidden behind:

- trusted intermediaries;
- private workflows;
- authenticated systems;
- institutional permissions;
- bespoke introductions;
- sensitive data exchange;
- low-frequency decision events.

Conversely, a smaller market can be experimentally attractive when actors self-identify through observable pre-decision behavior.

## Updated opportunity anatomy

A more complete opportunity structure is now:

```text
ECONOMICALLY CONSEQUENTIAL UNCERTAINTY
× RECOVERABLE INFORMATION
× INADEQUATE EXISTING RESOLUTION
× RESOLUTION FEASIBILITY
× ACCESSIBLE DECISION SURFACE
× OBSERVABLE EFFECT
× PLAUSIBLE VALUE CAPTURE
```

Any factor approaching zero can make the opportunity unattractive even when the others are strong.

This is conceptual multiplication, not a numerical scoring model.

## Implication for RADAR candidate generation

The previous flow often looked like:

```text
ASYMMETRY
→ ACTOR
→ RESOLUTION
→ WHERE CAN WE REACH THEM?
```

The new learning suggests testing the reverse direction as a complementary discovery policy:

```text
ACCESSIBLE PRE-DECISION BEHAVIOR
→ ACTOR
→ DECISION
→ UNCERTAINTY
→ ASYMMETRY
→ RECOVERABILITY
→ EXACT RESOLUTION GAP
```

The key is not merely to find visible people or complaints. The surface must expose actors **before the economic consequence occurs**, while the decision can still be influenced.

## Relation to earlier experiments

Several earlier branches now align around this distinction:

- Appliance keyword research showed that observable search behavior can exist while the exact asymmetry remains weak or ambiguous.
- Review-derived discovery showed that visible dissatisfaction often appears after purchase, when decision recoverability has decayed.
- Structural-change discovery produced stronger asymmetries but the tariff branch showed that those decisions may still live inside institutional workflows.

The next discovery experiment should therefore search for the intersection rather than optimizing any one property in isolation.

## New working prior

> **Prefer asymmetries whose affected actors reveal themselves at an accessible pre-decision surface.**

This is a prior, not a hard filter. It should be tested rather than institutionalized prematurely.

A particularly attractive topology for the Engine is:

```text
decision uncertainty
→ observable behavior
→ self-identifying actor
→ structured inputs
→ immediate resolution
→ observable response
```

This can compress discovery, distribution, and experimentation into the same surface.

## Parking rule reinforced

A demonstrated asymmetry plus a correct resolution does not justify continued investment.

> **A real asymmetry with a working resolution can still be a poor opportunity if its interface with reality is too expensive.**

The correct action is to PARK rather than rescue by broadening actor, geography, product scope, or channel assumptions.

The Canadian counter-tariff branch may be revisited only if a legitimate low-friction importer-access channel becomes available or a materially new distribution fact changes the constraint.
