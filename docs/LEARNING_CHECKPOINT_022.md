# Learning Checkpoint — Spec 022

**Checkpoint date:** 2 September 2026  
**Scope:** Spec 022  
**Relationship:** Continuation of learning checkpoints through Spec 021.

## Outcome

Spec 022 returned **C — NO SURVIVOR, DISCOVERY STILL INFORMATIVE**.

Signal-native discovery generated nine traceable hypotheses from three public-signal families. Several were materially less obvious than the familiar-problem candidates in Spec 021, but none crossed the residual-opportunity gate.

```text
SPEC 021
familiar candidate generation
→ strong evaluation
→ 0 survivors

SPEC 022
signal-native candidate generation
→ less obvious hypotheses
→ strong evaluation
→ 0 survivors
```

This means candidate generation improved in originality and traceability, but not yet in commercial survival.

## 1. Signal-native discovery worked

The evidence-first process successfully generated hypotheses that were not merely brainstormed first and justified later.

Each candidate could be traced through:

```text
RAW OBSERVATION
      ↓
OBSERVED PATTERN
      ↓
ACTOR
      ↓
DECISION
      ↓
MISSING INFORMATION
      ↓
ECONOMIC CONSEQUENCE
      ↓
OPPORTUNITY HYPOTHESIS
```

This strengthens the policy:

> **Evidence should generate the hypothesis, not merely decorate it.**

## 2. Structural change was the strongest discovery family

Among the three tested signal families:

- behavioral exhaust produced real but often post-incident friction;
- structural change produced the least obvious and most configuration-specific hypotheses;
- regulator-documented market friction produced economically meaningful hypotheses but sometimes exposed variables that remained unavailable at provider level.

Structural change therefore receives a **temporary research priority**, not a permanent source ranking.

The reason appears to be that fresh rules often contain:

```text
effective date
+ affected actor/configuration
+ changed obligation
+ transition condition
+ economic consequence
```

which makes candidate reconstruction unusually concrete.

## 3. Behavioral exhaust frequently appears after recoverability has decayed

Several payment-dispute observations surfaced only after the decisive evidence or institutional state had already become unavailable.

This suggests a stronger behavioral-source question:

> **Does the signal appear before or during the economic decision, while the relevant uncertainty is still recoverable?**

Post-incident complaints remain useful evidence of consequence and failure modes, but may be weak opportunity generators when the missing variable cannot be reconstructed in time.

## 4. Public friction can still depend on private state

The cross-party payment-dispute hypothesis demonstrated an important failure mode:

```text
PUBLICLY VISIBLE FRICTION
        ↓
REAL ECONOMIC CONSEQUENCE
        ↓
BUT
        ↓
DECISIVE STATE HELD PRIVATELY
BY MULTIPLE INSTITUTIONS
```

A public signal is not enough if the resolution requires inaccessible transaction, network, provider, or institutional state.

Preserve:

> **Public evidence of a problem does not imply public recoverability of the answer.**

## 5. Novelty and opportunity quality remain separate

Spec 022 produced several intersectional or surprising hypotheses, including old-stock environmental-claim treatment and contradictory bank/merchant dispute state.

They still failed.

Therefore:

```text
NOVEL / SURPRISING
≠
COMMERCIALLY ATTRACTIVE
```

Novelty may improve exploration but must not receive positive economic weighting by itself.

## 6. Fresh rules create both gaps and response waves

The strongest candidate—old-stock environmental-claim triage—was generated by a current regulatory transition and appeared highly specific and recoverable.

The exact-resolution audit then found multiple commercial products already targeting substantially the same decision.

This reveals a new temporal feature of opportunities:

```text
RULE / MARKET CHANGE
        ↓
UNCERTAINTY APPEARS
        ↓
EARLY WORKAROUNDS
        ↓
COMMERCIAL RESPONSE
        ↓
RESOLUTION MARKET CONSOLIDATES
```

A public change may create a genuine information asymmetry while simultaneously attracting rapid incumbent or startup response.

The relevant question is not merely:

> Is the rule new?

It is:

> **Is the decision gap still commercially unresolved?**

## 7. Opportunity windows become a working concept

Spec 022 motivates the concept of an **opportunity window**:

> The period between creation of economically consequential uncertainty and adequate consolidation of the market's response.

Conceptually:

```text
announcement / publication
        ↓
uncertainty created
        ↓
actors begin making decisions
        ↓
resolution supply emerges
        ↓
market becomes adequately served
```

The most attractive timing may be after the decision problem becomes concrete but before resolution supply matures.

This is a working research concept, not yet a domain object, score, or architecture component.

## 8. Candidate generation bottleneck moved again

After Spec 021 the dominant question was whether RADAR could generate less obvious hypotheses.

Spec 022 showed that it can.

The bottleneck now appears closer to:

> **Can RADAR identify unresolved decision gaps early enough that adequate commercial resolution has not yet consolidated?**

```text
CANDIDATE GENERATION
specificity / novelty        strengthened

EVALUATION
cheap rejection              strengthened

TIMING / PRE-CONSOLIDATION
                              ???
```

## 9. Tentative source prior

Current evidence supports a temporary research prior:

```text
HIGHER CURRENT PRIORITY
fresh rules / transitions
regulator-documented friction
configuration-changing events

LOWER CURRENT PRIORITY
generic post-incident complaints
```

This is not a permanent source hierarchy. It should be revised when new experiments contradict it.

## 10. What is supported vs unproven

**Supported:** signal-native discovery produces more traceable and less obvious hypotheses than the Spec 021 familiar-problem batch.

**Not established:** signal-native candidates have higher commercial survival rates.

**Supported:** structural transitions can generate highly specific decision hypotheses.

**Not established:** regulatory change is a durable high-yield source of opportunities.

**Supported:** exact-resolution search remains effective against less obvious hypotheses.

**Not established:** public-rule changes can routinely be detected before solution markets respond.

**Supported:** some visible information problems fail because the decisive state is privately held.

**Not established:** a general recoverability classifier should be automated.

**Supported:** freshness alone is insufficient; a newly changed rule may already have mature commercial resolution.

**Not established:** opportunity-window timing can be measured reliably enough to score or automate.

## 11. Next empirical question

> **Can RADAR identify economically consequential decision gaps shortly after a public rule, policy, platform, product, or market change—before adequate commercial resolution has consolidated?**

This motivates Spec 023 — Pre-Consolidation Opportunity Discovery.
