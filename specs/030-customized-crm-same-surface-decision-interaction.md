# Spec 030 — Customized CRM Same-Surface Decision Interaction

## Status

FORGE behavioral interaction experiment.

This spec advances the fixed CRM case from disposable resolution production into one real actor interaction on the same public surface where the decision was originally exposed.

Do not broaden into market validation, outreach campaigns, lead generation, or commercialization.

## Context

Spec 028 discovered a customized-CRM stay / upgrade / negotiate / migrate opportunity by starting from an accessible pre-decision surface.

Spec 029 then produced a decision-ready disposable resolution for the fixed 12-person customized-Salesforce case and demonstrated:

- resolution production;
- evidence discipline;
- explicit uncertainty;
- decision-space reduction.

The next uncertainty is behavioral:

> **When the existing decision resolution is presented to the actor in the same public surface where the decision was exposed, does it measurably change their understanding, decision framing, confidence, shortlist, or next action?**

This is not a test of willingness to pay or market demand.

## Fixed actor and surface

Use only the original public CRM thread from Specs 028–029 and the actor who created it.

Do not substitute another participant.

Do not move the interaction to email, LinkedIn, direct message, Slack, Discord, a call, or any private channel.

The experiment specifically tests **same-surface interaction**.

## Permission and execution boundary

This experiment includes one public reply to the original thread.

Codex must **not post anything unless the repository owner explicitly authorizes the public interaction in the execution turn**.

If explicit authorization is absent, stop before posting and return verdict **D — INTERACTION NOT EXECUTED / AUTHORIZATION REQUIRED**.

Do not infer authorization from the existence of this spec.

Do not create accounts, bypass platform restrictions, automate posting, or use alternate identities.

## Experimental objective

Test one mechanism:

```text
PUBLIC DECISION SIGNAL
→ DECISION RESOLUTION
→ SAME-SURFACE DELIVERY
→ OBSERVABLE ACTOR RESPONSE
→ POSSIBLE DECISION EFFECT
```

The primary question is not whether the actor says "thanks."

It is whether the interaction produces evidence that the resolution changed any decision-relevant state.

## Natural baseline

The original post and actor follow-up form the pre-intervention baseline.

Reconstruct the baseline before drafting the reply.

Capture only what was observable before intervention:

- options under consideration;
- framing of the problem;
- stated costs and constraints;
- unresolved uncertainties;
- apparent next actions;
- confidence language if explicit;
- requests/questions posed to the community.

Do not infer internal intent that was not stated.

## Intervention content

The public reply should be a compact decision-oriented version of the Spec 029 artifact, not a dump of the full Markdown brief.

It should:

1. acknowledge the actor's actual decision;
2. present the narrowed serious option set;
3. distinguish known facts from public facts and unresolved private workflow facts;
4. surface the most decision-sensitive discriminator;
5. avoid pretending to know the "best CRM";
6. give a small number of concrete next actions;
7. invite correction or challenge;
8. ask for one high-information follow-up only if useful.

The reply must remain useful even if the actor never engages further.

## Preferred interaction shape

The core message should approximately achieve this structure without mechanically copying wording:

```text
Your decision appears narrower than "which CRM is best?"

The serious branches look like:
- Professional + API
- Enterprise
- HubSpot Enterprise, conditional on workflow parity

The decision seems to hinge on whether your highest-value custom workflow can be preserved or reproduced, plus the actual commercial terms Salesforce will offer.

The one thing I would test before committing is [critical discriminator].

If useful, describe one anonymized critical workflow as:
objects → trigger → state changes → integration → exception → expected output.
That would make the migration/stay comparison much more concrete.
```

Do not use sales language, promotional framing, a product name, a landing page, or a call to action unrelated to the actor's decision.

## Artifact disclosure

Do not present the Spec 029 artifact as professional consultancy, certified advice, or a product.

It may be referenced as an analysis prepared from the information the actor posted and current public vendor documentation.

Do not claim guarantees.

## Interaction ethics

The reply must be:

- transparent;
- non-deceptive;
- non-promotional;
- proportionate;
- specific to the public facts the actor volunteered;
- respectful of privacy;
- free of pressure to continue the interaction.

Do not reproduce sensitive-looking commercial details unnecessarily if summarizing them is sufficient.

Do not ask for confidential CRM exports, customer records, credentials, contracts, or proprietary business data.

If asking for a workflow description, explicitly request an anonymized description only.

## Primary behavioral signals

After posting, observe only public responses on the same thread.

Strong signals include the actor:

- correcting a material assumption;
- narrowing or changing the shortlist;
- reframing the decision;
- identifying the critical workflow;
- supplying an anonymized acceptance scenario;
- deciding to obtain a specific quote;
- deciding to run a parity test;
- deciding to defer commitment pending verification;
- reporting a confidence change with reason;
- explicitly stating that the resolution changed what they will do next.

These are more important than praise or generic engagement.

## Secondary signals

Secondary but weaker evidence includes:

- asking a specific follow-up question;
- requesting clarification;
- challenging evidence;
- engaging with one of the decision-sensitive unknowns;
- sharing an additional bounded case fact that makes the decision more resolvable.

## Weak signals

Do not overinterpret:

- upvotes;
- generic thanks;
- thread views;
- unrelated commenter activity;
- agreement without any decision-relevant change;
- silence where exposure is unverified.

## No-response interpretation

No response is **not** automatically evidence that the resolution created no value.

Classify the failure mode as far as evidence permits:

```text
DELIVERY UNKNOWN
Could not establish that the actor saw the reply.

ATTENTION FAILURE
Exposure is plausible but no engagement followed.

COMPREHENSION / TRUST FAILURE
Actor responded but did not accept, understand, or use the framing.

VALUE FAILURE
Actor understood the resolution but indicated it did not improve the decision.
```

Do not collapse these into one negative result.

## Observation window

Use a bounded observation window after posting.

Default: **72 hours** from the public reply unless the user explicitly instructs otherwise.

If the platform exposes reliable evidence that the actor responded earlier, evaluate immediately.

Do not repeatedly bump the thread, post follow-ups, or contact the actor elsewhere during the window.

If the interaction remains unresolved at the end of the window, report the observed state without inventing intent.

## One-reply limit

Post at most **one initial public reply** under this spec.

If the actor responds, one direct follow-up reply is allowed only when necessary to answer a concrete question or clarify the proposed decision test.

Do not turn the experiment into a conversation funnel.

Do not exceed two total project replies under this spec.

## Measurement frame

Compare the actor's public pre-intervention baseline with the public post-intervention state.

Assess separately:

### M1 — Understanding

Did the actor demonstrate a clearer representation of the decision or material unknowns?

### M2 — Framing

Did the actor move from broad vendor comparison toward a smaller decision structure or critical discriminator?

### M3 — Shortlist

Did the actor eliminate, add, or reprioritize an option?

### M4 — Confidence

Did the actor express a reasoned increase or decrease in confidence?

### M5 — Next action

Did the actor commit to or report a concrete next step such as obtaining a quote, defining the workflow, or running a parity test?

### M6 — Challenge / trust

Did the actor validate, correct, reject, or materially challenge the analysis?

A successful experiment does not require all six.

## Decision-effect threshold

Classify a **material decision effect** only if at least one of the following occurs:

- option set changes;
- decision framing changes materially;
- a decision-sensitive unknown becomes explicit and prioritized;
- a specific next action changes;
- the actor provides new information specifically in response to the resolution that makes the decision more tractable.

Generic appreciation alone does not qualify.

## Resolution integrity during interaction

Do not alter the core Spec 029 resolution merely to gain engagement.

If the actor corrects a factual assumption, update the interpretation transparently.

Do not defend the original artifact for consistency's sake.

Reality overrides the artifact.

## Optional follow-up question

If one follow-up question is useful, prefer the highest-information unresolved item from Spec 029:

> Please describe one anonymized highest-value workflow as: objects/records involved → trigger → expected state changes → integration involved → exception path → expected output.

Ask this only if it naturally follows from the interaction.

Do not require it as a condition of giving value.

## Verdicts

### A — MATERIAL DECISION EFFECT OBSERVED

The actor was exposed and public response provides evidence of at least one material decision effect under the defined threshold.

This demonstrates the same-surface resolution mechanism for one actor.

It does **not** validate market demand.

### B — INTERACTION OCCURRED, EFFECT AMBIGUOUS

The actor engaged, but the evidence does not clearly establish a material decision effect.

Identify exactly what was observed and what remains ambiguous.

Do not rescue with more interaction.

### C — INTERACTION OCCURRED, NO MATERIAL EFFECT OBSERVED

The actor was credibly exposed and engaged enough to demonstrate understanding, but the resolution did not materially improve framing, shortlist, prioritized uncertainty, or next action.

This is meaningful evidence against the resolution mechanism for this case.

### D — INTERACTION INVALID / NOT EXECUTED

Use when the experiment cannot observe its target behavior, including:

- no explicit authorization to post;
- platform/access failure;
- thread unavailable or locked;
- actor exposure cannot be established and no response occurs;
- another execution failure prevents interpretation.

Do not call D evidence against value.

## Governing principles

> **FORGE makes something capable of interacting with reality.**

> **A working resolution is not evidence of value until it changes something for a real decision-maker.**

> **Same-surface discovery is valuable only if same-surface intervention actually works.**

> **No response is not equivalent to no value unless exposure and comprehension are observable.**

> **Reality overrides the artifact.**

> **One interaction can demonstrate a mechanism; it cannot validate a market.**
