READY FOR EXECUTION

## Why this experiment exists

Experiments 030–045 materially improved how Asymmetry Engine reasons about actor-facing experimentability.

The project now distinguishes:

```text
SURFACE ACCESS
≠ INTERVENTION PERMISSION
≠ DELIVERY
≠ ACTOR EXPOSURE

SEMANTIC ENGAGEMENT
≠ VERIFIED HUMAN COGNITIVE CHANGE

MESSAGE AUTHORSHIP
≠ ACCOUNT / CHANNEL IDENTITY
≠ DECISION AUTHORITY
≠ ACCOUNTABILITY

DECISION-STATE REFINEMENT
≠ STATED NEXT ACTION
≠ OBSERVED DOWNSTREAM ACTION
≠ ECONOMIC EFFECT
≠ VALUE CAPTURE
```

Interaction evidence is represented as a non-rigid, hypothesis-dependent ledger rather than a mandatory pipeline.

The project should now return to its core economic question and test the aligned policy prospectively on fresh opportunity discovery.

This experiment must not manufacture an INTERACT case. RADAR should discover, discriminate, and stop naturally. A candidate advances only if evidence earns advancement.

## Primary question

Can current RADAR policy discover a fresh economically consequential decision whose residual uncertainty survives early fatal gates and exact-resolution competition and whose eventual hypothesis has a credible, observable effect path?

## Secondary questions

1. Does the aligned interaction policy improve candidate experimentability before FORGE work is spent?
2. Which fatal gates now dominate fresh candidate rejection?
3. Does actor-first discovery continue to reduce inaccessible-actor failures?
4. Does early exact-resolution checking continue to prevent unnecessary deep research?
5. Does the interaction evidence ledger expose measurement-invalid candidates before intervention design?
6. Does any candidate earn a bounded FORGE handoff?

## Non-goals

- whether GitHub is better than Reddit;

- human versus AI authorship prevalence;
- willingness to pay;
- pricing;
- revenue;

- autonomous outreach;

## Research budget

- Target active research: 30–45 minutes.
- Hard ceiling: 50 active minutes.
- Incremental paid spend: €0.
- External interventions: 0.
- Maximum candidates deepened: 3.
- Stop early when every candidate has a decisive disposition or one candidate has a single bounded discriminator suitable for the next experiment.

Use prospective active-work timing.

Do not fill the budget merely because time remains.

## Surface-family requirement

Inspect at least 5 materially different public signal families unless decisive evidence appears earlier.

Prefer a mix such as:

- open-source project governance / issue / proposal surfaces;
- standards / technical working groups;
- public procurement / tenders / notices;
- regulator / public-agency consultations or implementation decisions;
- product support / technical communities;
- founder / operator communities where platform rules permit research;
- public Q&A / professional forums;
- public company / vendor migration, deprecation, pricing, or implementation discussions;
- eligibility / application / compliance workflows;
- marketplaces or other public decision surfaces.

At least 3 inspected families should not be Reddit and should not be generic web search result pages.

Generic search may locate native surfaces but does not itself count as a signal family.

Do not force candidate counts per family.

## Signal-native requirement

A candidate must originate in a concrete current signal, not a generic market idea.

Examples of acceptable native signals:

- an actor asking for help with a still-open consequential choice;
- a live proposal with unresolved sequencing or implementation choice;
- a public procurement decision with unresolved option structure;
- a migration/deprecation decision exposing a real actor and consequence;
- a standards or governance decision with identifiable decision participants;
- a current eligibility/application decision where the actor and residual uncertainty are public enough to resolve legitimately.

Reject generic categories such as “companies struggle with CRM” or “grant applications are hard.”

## Candidate anatomy

A qualifying candidate should be representable as:

```text
SIGNAL
  ↓
AUTHORITATIVE ACTOR / DECISION CHANNEL
  ↓
STILL-CHANGEABLE DECISION
  ↓
ECONOMIC CONSEQUENCE
  ↓
MATERIAL UNCERTAINTY
  ↓
RECOVERABLE INFORMATION
  ↓
EXACT-RESOLUTION GAP
  ↓
POTENTIALLY BETTER RESOLUTION
  ↓
HYPOTHESIS-RELEVANT OBSERVABILITY
```

This is an analysis scaffold, not a mandatory runtime pipeline.

A fatal failure may stop the candidate immediately.

## Gate 1 — Actor / decision channel

Identify the narrowest authoritative actor or decision channel supported by evidence.

Classify decision authority provisionally as:

- `DIRECT`
- `STRONG`
- `INFERRED`
- `UNKNOWN`

This classification is scoped to the decision object. It does not imply organizational authority, implementation authority, or verified human authorship.

Prefer `DIRECT` or `STRONG` candidates.

`UNKNOWN` authority is normally a kill unless the next cheap observation could resolve it before material deepening.

Do not infer human/AI authorship from style.

## Gate 2 — Still-changeable decision

The candidate must involve a real decision that is not already completed, expired, contractually closed, merged, shipped, awarded, purchased, migrated, or otherwise no longer changeable in the relevant sense.

Historical frustration is not a candidate.

A support incident with no remaining choice is not a candidate.

## Gate 3 — Economic consequence

Identify what is plausibly at stake:

- money;
- time;
- implementation/rework cost;
- operational risk;
- downtime;
- opportunity cost;
- procurement cost;
- compliance cost;
- material resource allocation;
- other concrete economic consequence.

Do not require precise market sizing.

Kill weakly consequential cases early.

## Gate 4 — Material uncertainty

State the actual unresolved question.

A candidate is not attractive merely because the topic is complex.

The uncertainty must be capable of changing the decision.

## Gate 5 — Recoverability

Classify recoverability:

- `HIGH`
- `MEDIUM`
- `LOW`
- `UNKNOWN`

Identify whether decisive information is:

- publicly recoverable;
- recoverable through bounded legitimate research;
- actor-held and plausibly obtainable through a future legitimate interaction;
- private/inaccessible;
- physical/inspection-dependent;
- professionally qualified or regulated beyond current competence.

Kill cases where decisive evidence is inaccessible and no legitimate bounded path exists.

## Gate 6 — Exact-resolution competition

Before substantial deepening, check whether an adequate existing resolution already performs the actor's exact job.

Compare where possible:

```text
ACTOR × DECISION × INPUTS × RESOLUTION × OUTPUT × TIMING
```

Possible exact resolvers include:

- official guidance;
- calculators;
- eligibility tools;
- migration guides;
- existing issue/proposal answers;
- comparison tools;
- decision trees;
- public professional guidance;
- product/vendor documentation;
- another current solution already sufficient for the decision.

Kill when the exact job is already adequately resolved.

Do not confuse information dispersion with a residual resolution gap.

## Gate 7 — Residual resolution gap

For survivors, state precisely what a better resolution would add.

Examples:

- integrate evidence currently separated across authoritative sources;
- resolve a sequencing dependency;
- expose one dominant discriminator;
- compare bounded options against actor-specific constraints;
- recover actor-held state that changes the choice;
- transform unstructured uncertainty into a decision-ready next test.

If the proposed value is merely “summarize information better,” challenge whether that changes the decision.

## Gate 8 — Interaction placement

If eventual interaction might matter, classify the plausible placement using the existing topology dimension:

- `SAME`
- `ADJACENT`
- `SEPARATE`
- `NONE / NOT NEEDED`
- `UNKNOWN`

This is one dimension only. Do not score it.

Do not infer permission from surface availability.

## Gate 9 — Hypothesis-relevant interaction evidence ledger

For each candidate deepened far enough that actor-facing validation could become useful, declare only the evidence states relevant to the likely future hypothesis.

Available ledger states include:

```text
surface access
intervention permission
delivery
exposure
semantic engagement
account/channel identity
decision authority/accountability
authorship provenance
decision-state refinement
stated next action
observed downstream action
economic effect
value capture
```

Do not require every state.

For each relevant state classify:

- `OBSERVABLE`
- `INDIRECTLY OBSERVABLE`
- `ACTOR-REPORT DEPENDENT`
- `NOT OBSERVABLE`
- `UNKNOWN`

Explain the evidence mechanism briefly.

### Exposure rule

Same-surface delivery is not exposure.

A substantive authoritative response may itself establish exposure when logically sufficient.

If silence would leave exposure UNKNOWN and the target hypothesis requires exposure, record that measurement limitation before intervention design.

### Authorship provenance

Use only disclosed or directly supported provenance:

- `HUMAN VERIFIED`
- `AI-ASSISTED DISCLOSED`
- `DELEGATED AGENT DISCLOSED`
- `AUTOMATED DISCLOSED`
- `UNKNOWN`

Do not infer authorship from style.

Unknown authorship is not itself a kill when the hypothesis concerns an authoritative decision channel rather than verified human cognition.

## Gate 10 — Effect observability

State the cheapest credible evidence that would show the resolution changed something relevant.

Possible evidence:

- actor explicitly accepts/rejects/refines the decision framing;
- proposal or issue disposition changes;
- actor supplies missing state that changes the resolution;
- documented configuration/test result changes;
- shortlist or stated next action changes;
- a public downstream artifact changes;
- an observable transaction/action occurs where relevant and legitimate.

Do not treat:

- publication;
- page views;
- comment counts;
- generic reactions;
- semantic-looking third-party chatter;

as decision effect unless the hypothesis specifically makes them relevant.

## Gate 11 — Synthetic-evidence contamination check

For candidates relying on public interaction, ask proportionally:

- Could a semantically plausible response occur without representing the authoritative decision process?
- Is the response attributable to the relevant decision channel?
- Would unknown authorship materially weaken the intended claim?
- What downstream evidence would be required before claiming real-world or economic effect?

Do not presume contamination occurred.

Do not perform bot detection.

The purpose is to constrain the claim, not identify hidden AI use.

## Gate 12 — Control feasibility

Before any candidate can be handed to FORGE, classify future control feasibility where relevant:

- `PASS`
- `CONDITIONAL`
- `REVIEW REQUIRED`
- `BLOCK`

Consider at minimum:

- platform/community rules;
- intervention legitimacy;
- privacy/sensitive information;
- professional/regulatory boundaries;
- authorization requirements;
- external spend;
- irreversible commitments.

No external action is authorized by Spec 046 regardless of classification.

## Candidate dispositions

Each candidate must end in exactly one disposition:

### KILL — ACTOR / AUTHORITY

No sufficiently identifiable authoritative actor/channel.

### KILL — DECISION CLOSED

Decision no longer changeable.

### KILL — CONSEQUENCE

Economic consequence insufficient.

### KILL — UNCERTAINTY

No decision-sensitive residual uncertainty.

### KILL — RECOVERABILITY

Decisive evidence unavailable through a legitimate bounded path.

### KILL — EXACT RESOLUTION

Adequate existing resolution already performs the exact job.

### KILL — RESOLUTION VALUE

No credible better resolution likely to change the decision.

### KILL — EXPERIMENTABILITY

The intended effect cannot be observed well enough for the hypothesis.

### KILL — CONTROL

Legitimate/authorized future testing is blocked or disproportionate.

### HOLD — ONE BOUNDED DISCRIMINATOR

One cheap unresolved observation could determine whether the candidate survives.

### SURVIVE — FORGE HANDOFF

All necessary current gates pass sufficiently to justify one bounded resolution experiment.

Do not force a survivor.

## Deepening rule

Deepen at most 3 candidates.

Choose candidates for deepening based on surviving fatal gates and expected information value—not novelty, personal interest, or market size.

For each deepened candidate record:

1. exact decision;
2. actor/authority evidence;
3. economic consequence;
4. residual uncertainty;
5. recoverability;
6. exact-resolution findings;
7. proposed resolution delta;
8. likely future hypothesis;
9. relevant interaction ledger states;
10. cheapest effect evidence;
11. synthetic-contamination limitation where relevant;
12. control feasibility;
13. cheapest remaining discriminator;
14. final disposition.

## Success criteria

Experiment 046 is successful as a RADAR test if it produces high-information dispositions under the aligned policy.

Success does not require a survivor.

A strong result could be:

- one clean FORGE handoff;
- one bounded HOLD discriminator;
- or a well-supported rejection set that reveals the current dominant bottleneck.

## Verdicts

### A — FORGE SURVIVOR

At least one fresh candidate survives all necessary current gates and earns a bounded FORGE handoff.

### B — BOUNDED HOLD / HIGH-INFORMATION REJECTION

No full survivor, but one bounded discriminator or a high-information rejection set materially improves RADAR policy understanding.

### C — LOW-YIELD SEARCH

Valid execution, but candidate quality/evidence yield is too weak to support meaningful policy learning or a handoff.

### D — POLICY REGRESSION / OVERCONSTRAINT

Valid execution indicates the aligned policy materially suppresses useful candidates or imposes disproportionate research burden without corresponding discrimination.

### E — INVALID

Scope, timing, controls, freshness, external-action, or repository-integrity requirements were violated.
