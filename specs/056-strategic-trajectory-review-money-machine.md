# Spec 056 — Strategic Trajectory Review: From Research Engine to Money Machine

## Status
READY FOR EXECUTION

## Type
Repository-only strategic review and Phase II operating-regime decision. No external research, fresh opportunity discovery, implementation, UI work, actor contact, monetization execution, policy deployment, or software changes.

## Baseline
Execute from synchronized canonical main at or after Experiment 055 published closure `c58bd5e95a9f9f78b3ebf798703767976b11fbe2`.

## Why this review exists
AE was conceived as the strongest candidate for producing low-maintenance/passive income: a money machine that searches business possibility space, resolves valuable asymmetries, learns what works, and compounds successful mechanisms.

After 55 experiments, AE has become a sophisticated research instrument with strong discrimination, evidence discipline, operator visibility and partial opportunity memory. It has not established economic effect, valid WTP/exchange, transaction, repeatability, or captured value.

The project may be locally optimizing evidence quality while globally failing its economic objective.

This review must decide how to correct trajectory and/or ambition before another opportunity experiment begins.

## Core strategic question
> What operating regime gives AE the highest credible probability of reaching repeatable, low-maintenance economic value quickly enough to justify continuing the project?

The desired output is not another insight. It is a decision about how the next approximately ten experiments will attempt to make money.

## Foundational diagnosis to challenge
Treat these as hypotheses, not conclusions:
- AE may have built a strong opportunity-discrimination capability, not yet proven strong commercial opportunity detection.
- RADAR is comparatively mature; FORGE / market conversion is weak.
- experimentation may have become a bottleneck rather than merely a learning mechanism.
- proxy demand validation may cost more than building a cheap transactable market test.
- zero-spend discipline may now be slowing learning.
- scientifically observable surfaces may have biased search away from commercially observable surfaces.
- generality of AE does not require generality of the Phase II business model.
- passive income may be a terminal optimization, not a sensible first-transaction constraint.
- current kill discipline may optimize false-positive avoidance at the cost of false negatives and commercial velocity.
- recent representation work improved operation but did not materially advance the economic frontier.

## Strategic decomposition
Do not treat “strategy” as one choice. Independently decide:

### 1. Search space
What should RADAR seek during Phase II?
Compare at minimum:
- broad/general asymmetry discovery;
- commercially constrained asymmetry discovery;
- narrow vertical/domain search;
- high-intent purchase/choice uncertainty;
- proven-market adaptation: begin with existing transaction streams and find underserved niche/geography/audience/workflow variants.

### 2. Monetization substrate
How should discovered value be captured?
Compare at minimum:
- affiliate/referral;
- lead generation;
- paid information/data;
- micro-product / one-purpose tool;
- micro-SaaS;
- manual paid resolution/service.

Explicitly consider the broader family:
**monetize decision resolution without owning fulfillment.**

### 3. Experimental regime
Compare:
- E: evidence-before-build;
- M: monetization-as-experiment.

Test the hypothesis:
> As AI-assisted execution cost falls, the cheapest valid discriminator may become the smallest transactable market treatment rather than another proxy-demand experiment.

## Minimum Transactable Resolution
Evaluate whether FORGE should be redefined or extended around:

> **Minimum Transactable Resolution (MTR): the cheapest sellable/referrable resolution that lets market behavior produce stronger economic evidence than further proxy validation.**

An MTR should be able to state:
- who pays or transacts;
- for what;
- through what mechanism;
- what they receive;
- delivery burden;
- exposure/traffic path;
- transaction telemetry;
- maximum downside.

Do not canonize MTR unless the review earns it.

## Fatal-gate versus market-test boundary
Determine which uncertainties should still be killed before market construction.

Challenge a candidate boundary such as:
- illegal/prohibited;
- unacceptable regulatory/control exposure;
- no plausible buyer/economic actor;
- inaccessible market/exposure path;
- adequate exact free/cheap resolver;
- unrecoverable information;
- downside/liability disproportionate to experiment;
then default toward market truth when building/exposing an MTR is cheaper than further validation.

The review must define where pre-validation should stop.

## Commercial observability
Assess whether Phase II should prefer opportunity topologies where:
- problem/decision is visible;
- buyer or transaction counterparty is identifiable;
- economic activity already exists;
- payment/referral topology is normal;
- qualified exposure is measurable;
- click/checkout/referral/transaction telemetry is available;
- fulfillment/support can be outsourced or minimized.

Preserve:
**no sales + verified qualified exposure ≠ no sales + unknown exposure.**

## Affiliate/referral hypothesis
Do not assume affiliate is the answer.

Test why it may fit AE:
- existing transaction stream;
- no owned inventory;
- merchant handles payment/fulfillment/returns/support substantially;
- AE can specialize in resolving purchase uncertainty;
- exposure/click/conversion/commission telemetry is comparatively observable;
- content/decision engine may transfer across niches.

Challenge:
- SEO/platform dependency;
- merchant/program instability;
- disclosure/compliance;
- content freshness;
- traffic acquisition;
- competition;
- commission economics;
- attribution loss;
- “passive” maintenance illusion.

Classify affiliate as reject / candidate / preferred substrate, with reasons.

## Content-engine / factory hypothesis
Test whether the economically important unit is not one opportunity but a reusable factory:

RADAR → decision opportunity → FORGE/MTR → structured decision model → reusable content/artifact engine → distribution → monetization → telemetry → learning.

Ask:
> What compounds after the first success?

Distinguish:
- opportunity economics;
- factory economics.

A strategy that earns once but teaches no reusable acquisition/resolution/monetization mechanism may be inferior to a smaller first win that makes the next launch cheaper.

## Proven-market adaptation challenge
Explicitly compare novel/residual discovery against:
> existing proven transaction → identify underserved variation → reproduce/adapt monetization topology.

Ask whether AE's differentiated role should be discovering the underserved variation rather than inventing the business model.

This is the strongest adversarial alternative to generalized RADAR and must not be dismissed because it appears less novel.

## Founder benchmark
Adversarially answer:
> Would a smart solo founder with €1,000 and ten experiments beat AE to first revenue? If yes, why?

Compare AE with a scrappy founder on:
- time to market;
- willingness to build before certainty;
- capital use;
- market exposure;
- iteration;
- proxy reliance;
- false-positive/false-negative posture;
- reuse/automation;
- evidence quality.

The target synthesis may be:
**scrappy founder velocity + machine-scale observation + unusually disciplined reasoning.**
Do not assume this synthesis is already achieved.

## Passive-income challenge
Separate:
- first economic proof;
- recurring economic mechanism;
- low-maintenance/passive terminal state.

Do not require the first test to be passive if that materially delays transaction evidence.
Do require the chosen substrate to have a plausible path toward low-maintenance recurring income if successful.

## Time-to-Market-Truth
Evaluate and, if justified, adopt a Phase II metric:

> **Time to Market Truth = elapsed/active time from opportunity admission to valid revealed-preference evidence under credible exposure.**

Define what qualifies as market truth for different substrates:
- affiliate/referral;
- lead-gen;
- paid information;
- direct payment;
- SaaS/tool.

Do not confuse a published page with exposure or zero revenue with a negative result absent a credible denominator.

The Phase II regime should aim to move this frontier, not merely produce additional upstream evidence.

## Economic accountability / stop condition
AE itself must face a falsifiable Phase II boundary.

Recommend a bounded stop/pivot rule expressed in opportunities/market tests, time, capital and/or qualified exposure.

Example form only:
> If N bounded market tests over X time and €Y produce no meaningful economic evidence under valid exposure, weaken/pivot the current Phase II strategy rather than extending research indefinitely.

Do not choose arbitrary values without reasoning, but do choose a usable rule.

## Phase II constitution
Produce a terse set of approximately 8–12 governing rules for future work.

Candidate ideas to test, not copy automatically:
- Market truth beats proxy evidence when cheaper to obtain.
- Existing transaction streams beat hypothesized demand.
- No meta-work unless it shortens a live economic path.
- €1 of valid capture is a new evidence class.
- Manual first is acceptable; recurring manual fulfillment is not the destination.
- No opportunity should sit many experiments away from economic exposure.
- Qualified exposure is required before interpreting no-sales evidence.
- Automate winners/repeated mechanics, not hypotheses.
- Preserve evidence rigor proportionally to consequence.

## Anti-sunk-cost rule

- increasing spend;
- suspending generalized search;
- holding parts of AE dormant.
