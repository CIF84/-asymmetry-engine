# Spec 059 — Phase II Proven-Flow Wedge Search

## Status
READY FOR EXECUTION

## Phase
AE Phase II — Economic Proof

## Purpose
Find at most one economically credible entry by starting from **proven monetized decision flows**, identifying a specific commercial wedge AE could test despite incumbents, and producing a launch-ready F1/F4 treatment or NONE.

This is the corrected successor to Experiment 058.

## Core correction from 058
Do **not** require informational novelty or an unsolved decision.

Existing competent solutions can be positive evidence that:
- the decision exists;
- buyers transact;
- capture topology works;
- acquisition/distribution surfaces exist.

The commercial question is:

> **Why could AE plausibly win a small piece of this existing economic flow?**

A candidate fails when no specific economically testable wedge is visible cheaply—not merely because an incumbent already solves the problem.

## Required outcome
Exactly one:

### Q — QUALIFY
One decision flow has:
- proven monetization;
- a specific commercial wedge;
- viable F1 and F4 paths;
- legitimate measurable exposure;
- bounded truthful treatment;
- acceptable control/downside.

Return a launch-ready paired treatment and stop for explicit human authorization.

### N — NONE
Bounded search finds no qualifier. Stop without widening scope or creating a research backlog.

### E — INVALID
Method/scope/integrity failure.

No “promising, continue researching” state.

## Search unit
A candidate is:

> **[proven monetized decision flow] + [specific decision-owner segment/job] + [specific wedge hypothesis].**

Not:
- an industry;
- an unsolved information gap;
- a product category alone;
- a generic affiliate niche;
- a generic service.

## Search order
For each candidate:

1. **Proven flow first** — establish that money already changes hands and at least one relevant capture model is visibly commercialized.
2. **Decision job** — identify the recurring decision that precedes or shapes that flow.
3. **Incumbent resolution** — understand what existing providers already do well.
4. **Wedge hypothesis** — identify one specific reason AE could plausibly win some transactions despite incumbents.
5. **F1/F4 feasibility** — test both capture paths.
6. **Exposure/control/economics** — test whether a bounded market treatment is possible.

Do not begin with “what information is missing?”

## Search breadth
Inspect **4–6 candidates maximum**.

Spend enough bounded effort to reach a concrete wedge, not merely identify competitors.

- Target 8–15 active minutes per serious candidate.
- Reject earlier when a fatal contradiction is decisive.
- Stop on first clear qualifier.
- If six candidates fail, return N.
- Do not retain ranked near-misses as automatic continuation.

Target total active time: 45–90 minutes. Hard ceiling: 120. External spend €0.

## Candidate sourcing
Fresh public web research is authorized.

Prefer proven flows with:
- recurring transaction volume;
- visible F1-like and/or F4-like monetization;
- identifiable decision owners;
- meaningful transaction value;
- public/licensed decision inputs;
- reachable audiences;
- low initial fulfillment burden;
- current terms/prices sufficient for treatment design.

Historical 058 candidates may be rediscovered naturally, but do not seed the search from them.

Do not preselect software.

## Gate P1 — Proven economic flow
Evidence that the underlying decision already supports real commercial activity.

Strong evidence can include:
- current paid offerings;
- active marketplaces;
- current merchant/referral/partner economics;
- public pricing/fees;
- credible transaction-volume evidence;
- other observable monetized behavior.

This establishes market existence, not AE advantage.

## Gate P2 — Specific recurring decision job
Define:
- decision owner;
- decision object/action;
- timing/context;
- economic consequence.

The job must recur across independent decision owners.

## Gate P3 — Commercial Wedge Gate
Replace 058 G3 entirely.

Require a **named, specific, economically testable wedge hypothesis** explaining why AE might capture some flow despite incumbents.

Potential wedge classes are illustrative, not exhaustive:
- underserved segment/audience;
- distribution/access advantage;
- materially better convenience or decision compression;
- localization;
- freshness/timeliness;
- cross-provider synthesis;
- workflow/integration;
- price/cost structure;
- trust/verification;
- a combination with explicit causal logic.

A wedge is not:
- “AI can do it”;
- “better UX” without mechanism;
- generic personalization;
- incumbent imperfection;
- lower price without credible cost basis;
- speculative novelty.

### Incumbent treatment
Existing solutions become:
- market proof;
- benchmarks;
- wedge challenges.

Ask:
> Does the incumbent already neutralize this exact wedge cheaply enough that AE has no credible reason to win?

If yes, reject.

Do not ask whether the overall problem is already solved.

## Gate P4 — Recoverable bounded resolution
AE can truthfully deliver the wedge from legitimate public/licensed information plus ordinary user-supplied inputs.

Ordinary user inputs (preferences, dates, budget, owned equipment, requirements) are allowed if the treatment can collect them normally.

Reject when the decisive input requires:
- unavailable supplier/actor-held evidence;
- privileged access;
- consequential guessing;
- unapproved contact before the treatment can function.

This explicitly corrects 058's tendency to treat all non-public inputs similarly.

## Gate P5 — F1 path
A coherent bounded offer exists where the decision owner pays AE directly.

Need:
- deliverable;
- scope;
- plausible price basis;
- payment route;
- credible fulfillment.

Do not require observed WTP before treatment.

## Gate P6 — F4 path
A legitimate third-party transaction-contingent route exists for the same decision flow.

Need current public evidence of:
- program/partner route;
- eligible economic event;
- broad eligibility/channel constraints;
- attribution/settlement mechanics sufficient for treatment design.

Do not enroll.

## Gate P7 — Comparable exposure
Prefer one legitimate audience/acquisition surface usable for both arms.

If exact common-channel parity is impossible, a **package-level F1/F4 comparison** may still qualify if:
- each channel is economically realistic for its topology;
- exposure is measurable;
- full acquisition costs are recorded;
- the report explicitly abandons pure topology-causal interpretation.

Do not reject a commercially realistic pair solely to preserve laboratory purity.

## Gate P8 — Measurable economic truth
Prospectively distinguish:
- qualified exposure;
- economic event;
- accepted/payable state;
- fulfillment;
- reversal/refund;
- settlement;
- full direct costs;
- human setup/delivery/exception time.

Negative interpretation requires valid exposure/event capture.

## Gate P9 — Bounded treatment
No durable product, SaaS, content factory, marketplace or automation system required.

Manual/disposable delivery is acceptable.

Treatment should be constructible after authorization with a small bounded effort.

## Gate P10 — Regulatory/control viability
No obvious legal, contractual, privacy, IP/data-rights, safety, regulated-advice or platform blocker makes the treatment illegitimate/disproportionate.

Bounded feasibility only; not legal advice.

## Gate P11 — Economic relevance
A successful event has plausible economic meaning after:
- payment/program fees;
- acquisition;
- bounded delivery;
- foreseeable support/exception burden.

No proven profitability required.

## Gate P12 — Honest falsifiability
Design distinguishes:
- bad wedge/economics;
- failed exposure/access;
- invalid measurement.

No “page existed, therefore no demand” inference.

## No proxy staircase
Once P1–P12 pass, do not insert:
- survey;
- stated-interest test;
- generic engagement test;
- “would you pay?”;
- actor-response experiment;
- public posting without economic telemetry.

The next discriminator is market exposure after human authorization.

### Comparison

- what intrinsically differs;
- whether comparison is topology-level or package-level;
- qualified exposure denominator;
- full-cost fields;
- observation and settlement horizons;
- stop conditions.

## Parameter discipline
Propose but do not activate:
- F1 price;
- F4 event value/range;
- maximum cash at risk;
- maximum human setup/delivery time;
- exposure denominator;
- observation horizon;
- settlement horizon.

Use current evidence/rationale. Avoid arbitrary precision.
