# Spec 060 — Distribution-First Economic Entry Search

## Status
READY FOR EXECUTION

## Phase
AE Phase II — Economic Proof

## Purpose
Find at most one economically credible opportunity by starting from **proven money flow and reachable qualified distribution**, then identifying a commercial wedge and selecting the capture topology that best fits the economics.

No capture topology is pre-required. F1–F11 are available mechanisms, not admission constraints.

## Primary question
> Can AE find one existing economic flow where qualified decision owners are credibly reachable, AE has a specific testable reason to win some flow, and one capture mechanism supports a cheap economically revealing treatment with attractive enough burden/dependency economics to justify human authorization?

## Required outcome
Exactly one:

### Q — QUALIFY
One opportunity passes all gates. Produce one launch-ready treatment using the best-fitting capture topology and stop for explicit human authorization.

### N — NONE
Bounded search produces no qualifier. Stop without widening scope or creating a continuation backlog.

### E — INVALID
Method/scope/integrity failure.

No “promising, research more” outcome.

## Search unit
A candidate is:

> **proven economic flow + specific decision owner/job + reachable distribution surface + commercial wedge + best-fitting capture mechanism**

Do not begin from a business-model label, F1/F4 requirement, unsolved information gap, or favorite sector.

## Search sequence

### S1 — Proven money flow
Establish that meaningful money already moves around the decision/activity.

Existing successful solutions are positive market evidence and competitors/benchmarks.

### S2 — Reachable qualified distribution
Before detailed wedge/treatment work, establish at least one legitimate route that plausibly reaches the decision-owner population.

Need public evidence of:
- relevant audience presence;
- route eligibility/permission in principle;
- measurable acquisition/exposure mechanics;
- plausible density sufficient for a small bounded treatment.

Do not require an owned audience.

Do not confuse:
- permission surface with audience density;
- impressions with qualified exposure;
- generic marketplace traffic with target access.

If distribution cannot be grounded cheaply, reject early.

### S3 — Specific commercial wedge
Identify one named reason AE could plausibly win some existing flow despite incumbents.

Possible mechanisms include segmentation, distribution, convenience, compression, localization, freshness, synthesis, workflow fit, trust/verification, price/cost structure or another explicit causal wedge.

“AI”, generic personalization, generic better UX and incumbent imperfection are insufficient.

Challenge the exact wedge against incumbents.

### S4 — Best-fitting capture topology
Only after S1–S3, evaluate the relevant F1–F11 mechanisms.

Choose the mechanism that minimizes total economic burden while preserving plausible capture.

Do **not** require multiple topologies for comparison.

Record why the chosen topology dominates plausible alternatives for this candidate.

### S5 — Economically revealing treatment
Design the cheapest truthful treatment that can generate genuine economic evidence.

No proxy staircase.

## Total-burden objective
Do not optimize B2B/B2C labels.

Evaluate candidate + topology on:
- acquisition effort/cost;
- number of counterparties/partners;
- approval/contract friction;
- fulfillment labor;
- support/exception burden;
- capital at risk;
- refund/liability/downside;
- platform concentration/dependency;
- attribution/settlement friction;
- maintenance/decay;
- cash-conversion time;
- automation potential after proof;
- recurrence;
- replication/factory potential.

Prefer higher recurring net economic output per unit of human attention, capital and dependency risk.

A B2B candidate may win if self-serve/low-friction. A B2C candidate may lose if distribution volume/support economics are poor.

## Search bounds
Inspect **4–6 candidates maximum**.

Candidate formation requires S1 + S2 evidence; do not spend detailed wedge effort on an ungrounded audience.

- Target 8–15 active minutes per serious candidate.
- Reject immediately on decisive early failure.
- Stop on first clear Q.
- Six failures → N.
- Target 45–90 minutes total; hard ceiling 120.
- External spend EUR 0.

## Gate E1 — Proven economic flow
Current evidence of paid transactions/offers/marketplaces/fees/rewards/other economic capture.

## Gate E2 — Qualified distribution
At least one legitimate measurable route with plausible target density.

This is an early fatal gate.

## Gate E3 — Recurring decision/event
The economic condition recurs across independent instances often enough to support repeated exposure/capture.

## Gate E4 — Commercial wedge
Specific causal reason AE might capture some flow despite incumbents.

No requirement for informational novelty.

## Gate E5 — Recoverable/executable contribution
AE can truthfully deliver/execute the wedge using legitimate information, ordinary user inputs and available authority.

If the topology is principal/own-account, require executable access rather than a hypothetical signal.

## Gate E6 — Capture topology fit
Select the best-fitting F1–F11 mechanism(s).

For each serious candidate:
- identify plausible mechanisms;
- choose one;
- explain why alternatives have worse burden/economics.

Hybrids are allowed only when economically necessary, not to preserve prior experiments.

## Gate E7 — Bounded economic treatment
First treatment requires no durable platform, audience-building program, SaaS, content factory or general infrastructure.

Manual/disposable treatment is acceptable.

## Gate E8 — Measurable economic truth
Can distinguish:
- qualified exposure/access;
- economic event;
- fulfillment/execution;
- payable/realized state;
- settlement/cash;
- direct costs;
- human effort;
- reversals/losses.

## Gate E9 — Control/regulatory viability
No obvious legal, contractual, privacy, IP/data, safety, regulated-advice or authority blocker makes the treatment disproportionate.

## Gate E10 — Economic relevance
A successful event could matter after realistic acquisition, fulfillment, fees, capital and burden.

No proven profitability required.

## Gate E11 — Path to lower burden
If first proof works, there is a plausible path to reduce recurring mechanical human effort.

Do not require automation now.

## Gate E12 — Honest falsifiability
Negative result can be separated from failed access/measurement.

## Distribution-source discipline
Research may inspect:
- marketplaces;
- search/advertising surfaces with public targeting/measurement;
- communities with explicit commercial/service rules;
- directories;
- merchant/partner ecosystems;
- existing transactional platforms;
- other legitimate acquisition/access surfaces.

No login/contact/posting/account creation.

A route requiring months of audience building fails E2 for this entry search unless that audience already exists and is legitimately controllable.

## Capture topology
Use the blind F1–F11 taxonomy as vocabulary, not a tournament.

Examples:
- F1 if direct self-serve payment is simplest;
- F4 if transaction-contingent capture removes fulfillment burden and access is strong;
- F5 if a controlled operation with measurable retained gains appears;
- F7 if lawful own-account execution has a credible cost-adjusted edge;
- other families when they genuinely fit.

Do not favor or penalize a topology because it was previously discussed.

## Preference handling
Human preferences are hypotheses about burden, not candidate-selection commands.

Do not encode “B2C preferred” or “B2B avoided.”

Instead use the total-burden objective above.

## No proxy staircase
After E1–E12 pass, do not insert:
- surveys;
- interviews;
- stated-interest checks;
- generic engagement tests;
- “would you pay?”;
- non-economic actor-response stages.

Next step after Q is explicit human authorization for the economic treatment.

## Parameter discipline
If Q, propose from current evidence:
- treatment;
- capture mechanism;
- acquisition route;
- price/event economics;
- maximum cash/capital at risk;
- maximum human time;
- qualified exposure/access denominator;
- observation/settlement horizon;
- event/full-cost ledger;
- stop conditions;
- obligations/reversibility.

No arbitrary precision.

- wedge;
- topology considered/chosen;
- first fatal gate;
- horizon/provenance;
- UNKNOWNs.

No continuation backlog.
