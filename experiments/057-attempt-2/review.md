## 7. F1 versus F4 topology conclusion
**SUPPORTED AS THE SMALLEST CURRENT EMPIRICAL CAPTURE QUESTION.**

The pair has a useful experimental property: the same underlying decision-resolution capability can potentially be monetized through two different claims:
- **F1:** the decision owner pays AE for the bounded contribution.
- **F4:** a third party pays AE contingent on a qualifying downstream exchange.

This is materially cleaner than comparing unrelated businesses.

The pair is not a pure laboratory isolation. Changing who pays changes offer price to the decision owner, contractual relationships, attribution, settlement timing and potentially trust/incentives. Those are intrinsic parts of the capture topology, not removable nuisance variables. The experiment should therefore compare **economic packages under a common decision job**, not claim a causal estimate of topology alone.

But this establishes **comparability**, not **opportunity quality**.

The report does not establish:

- a reachable qualified cohort;
- a legitimate common acquisition channel;

Therefore “B2B software choice” must remain an **illustrative common-job hypothesis**, not the fixed Phase II niche.

This is the main independent-review correction.

## 9. Acquisition-channel problem
The proposed F1/F4 head-to-head is only informative if the arms can share a sufficiently comparable qualified decision population.

If F1 relies on a marketplace's built-in buyer traffic while F4 relies on SEO/content traffic, observed differences would combine:
- capture topology;
- channel;
- audience;
- trust;
- intent;
- acquisition cost.

That can still compare two business packages, but it would not answer the narrower topology question.

The next packet should therefore first seek a **common legitimate acquisition surface** or explicitly classify the comparison as package-level if none exists. It must not spend several experiments manufacturing exposure merely to preserve the pair.
