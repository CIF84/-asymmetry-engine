# Experiment 057 Attempt 2 — Blind Economic Mechanism Space

## Provenance
Independent context-isolated GPT-6 Astra Extra High reasoning arm. Input was one self-contained factual capsule outside the AE repository/project context. No AE repository, web, prior strategic recommendations, or named candidate taxonomy were available. The full verbatim output is preserved in the human conversation; this durable artifact records its substantive result without adding post-reveal ranking.

## Final abstract mechanism taxonomy
The blind arm derived mechanisms from the economic claim producing payoff, not from product/business-model names.

- **F1 — Compensated provision of an output, capability, or action.** Another party pays for a delivered contribution. Recurrence comes from renewed needs/purchasers; main burdens are acquisition, fulfillment, QA, exceptions and support.
- **F2 — Delegated exploitation of reusable rights.** AE controls a reusable asset/permission that another operator commercializes. Recurrence comes from repeated use/licensees/renewals; key dependencies are transferable rights and capable operators.
- **F3 — Allocation of controlled access or scarce capacity.** Value comes from selling permission, capacity, priority or exposure to a controlled scarce resource. Durability of control and resource usefulness dominate.
- **F4 — Exchange-contingent intermediation and coordination.** AE helps independent parties complete an exchange and captures part of the resulting proceeds/savings. Matching, attribution, settlement and bypass risk dominate.
- **F5 — Retention of gains inside a controlled operation.** AE improves an operation whose cost savings/avoided losses/increased proceeds accrue to its owner. Requires decision authority and credible economic baseline.
- **F6 — Residual participation in productive cash flows.** AE owns a claim on surplus generated by an operation/productive asset. Capital, selection, governance and underlying asset quality dominate.
- **F7 — Own-account capture of changes in value or exchange differentials.** AE acts as principal and realizes a net differential on a controlled position. Requires executable access and an advantage surviving competition and transaction costs.
- **F8 — Compensation for advancing capital or providing liquidity over time.** Payoff is financing compensation net of funding cost/impairment. Requires deployable capital, underwriting and servicing.
- **F9 — Compensation for assuming contingent obligations.** Payoff is compensation for bearing specified adverse outcomes/performance obligations, net of claims and reserves. Tail/correlation risk dominates.
- **F10 — Qualification for externally allocated rewards or entitlements.** An external rule/selection process transfers value to qualifying conduct/evidence/contribution. Rule stability, qualification cost and allocator dependency dominate.
- **F11 — Voluntary support for continuing shared value.** Supporters fund continuing value without purchasing an enforceable commercial deliverable. Stewardship, supporter retention and relationship burden dominate.

Examples were treated only as illustrations after abstraction. Payment formulas such as subscriptions, commissions and royalties; production methods such as software/content/agents; information advantage; bundling/network effects; outsourcing; financing; portfolio wrappers; reputation/learning/optionality were rejected as separate elementary capture families.

## Completeness challenge
The provisional six-branch map was materially revised:
- **Added:** F10 external allocation; F11 voluntary support.
- **Merged:** information/decision/action provision into F1; matching/referral/coordination into F4; attention/priority/scarce access into F3; cost reduction/avoided loss/increased operating proceeds into F5.
- **Split:** reusable rights vs scarce access (F2/F3); productive residual ownership vs value differential (F6/F7); time/liquidity provision vs contingent risk bearing (F8/F9).

Missing-opposite checks explicitly tested capture without selling, without owning fulfillment, without a direct customer, without an audience, principal versus intermediary action, and transferring commercialization to another party.

## Structural objective-fit result
No winner was selected.

The blind arm identified conditional clusters:
- **Bounded initial learning / limited capital:** F1, F4, F5; F2 when usable rights already exist.
- **Low mature operating effort:** F2, F3, F6; potentially F8 when servicing/losses are controlled.
- **Repeated autonomous action:** F5, F7, F10 when action, authority, qualification and settlement are sufficiently stable.

Cross-cutting findings:
- lower labor can require more capital or stronger control rights;
- cheap first tests and easy mature operation are different advantages;
- recurring need does not guarantee recurring advantage;
- theoretical automation potential does not establish executable automation;
- replication can amplify common dependencies/failure modes.

## Evidence needed for discrimination
Six fact bundles were identified:
1. **Actual control and access.**
2. **Frequency of an economically unresolved condition.**
3. **Observable proceeds, effects and full costs.**
4. **Decay and exception burden.**
5. **Capital exposure through the full obligation period.**
6. **Independence and genuine reuse.**

The arm preserved these measurement distinctions:
- payment can establish exchange/capture while downstream benefit remains unknown;
- retained savings require a credible counterfactual;
- position returns must be separated from ordinary compensation for capital/risk before attributing advantage;
- rewards/contributions count only when received, usable and net of obligations;
- missing observations remain UNKNOWN;
- failed exposure is not failed demand.

Milestones remain distinct:
1. first valid economic proof;
2. repeatable economic mechanism;
3. low-maintenance recurring value;
4. portfolio/factory replication.

**No preferred economic mechanism was selected in the blind exercise.**
