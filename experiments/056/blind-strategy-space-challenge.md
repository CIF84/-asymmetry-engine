# Experiment 056 — Blind Strategy-Space Challenge

## Status
FROZEN BEFORE READING THE EXPERIMENT 056 STRATEGY RESULT

## Method
This challenge was constructed from AE's economic objective and repository evidence through Experiment 055, deliberately excluding the Experiment 056 result/conclusions. Public business-model literature was used only as a conceptual sanity check that value creation, delivery and capture are separable business-model choices; it did not determine the AE-specific ranking.

The purpose is contamination control: reconstruct plausible value-capture topology independently, then compare the frozen map with 056.

## Pre-056 economic facts
Repository evidence establishes:
- AE seeks repeatable captured economic value and ultimately low-maintenance portfolio income.
- Economic effect, WTP/exchange, transaction, repeatability and captured value remain unestablished.
- AE is comparatively strong at discrimination, recoverable-information analysis, exact-resolution challenge, bounded resolution construction, provenance/control and operator visibility.
- Actor-facing experiments repeatedly suffered from access, exposure and effect-observability distance.
- No repository evidence establishes that affiliate/referral, trading/direct exploitation, SaaS, lead generation, paid information or any other substrate is economically superior for AE.
- The existing operating model is historically oriented toward improving human decisions, but the economic objective does not logically require a human buyer.

## Independent value-capture topology

### T1 — Sell the resolution
AE finds/resolves uncertainty and sells the answer or resolution directly.

Examples:
- paid report/data;
- personalized analysis;
- manual decision service;
- API;
- one-purpose tool;
- subscription/SaaS.

Economic chain:
ASYMMETRY → RESOLUTION → CUSTOMER → PAYMENT

Strength:
direct attribution between delivered resolution and revenue.

Weakness:
requires customer acquisition, pricing, fulfillment/support and usually a buyer/payer relationship.

### T2 — Monetize a third-party decision/transaction
AE resolves a decision but another provider owns fulfillment; AE captures referral/lead/commission value.

Examples:
- affiliate/referral;
- lead generation;
- comparison/decision sites;
- transaction brokerage where lawful.

Economic chain:
ASYMMETRY → DECISION RESOLUTION → THIRD-PARTY TRANSACTION → COMMISSION/FEE

Strength:
can outsource inventory, fulfillment, payment and much support; naturally aligned with decision-resolution capability.

Weakness:
distribution, attribution, merchant/program dependency and commission economics can dominate.

### T3 — Directly exploit the asymmetry
AE uses its resolution to take an economic position/action itself rather than sell the information.

Examples in principle:
- trading;
- price/marketplace arbitrage;
- prediction/forecast-market positions where lawful;
- other lawful machine-executable economic discrepancies.

Economic chain:
ASYMMETRY → RESOLUTION → OWN ECONOMIC ACTION → P&L

Strength:
very short value-capture chain; monetization is intrinsic; recurrence and automation can be native; no external customer/WTP step.

Weakness:
capital at risk; noisy causal inference; statistical validation burden; competition/edge decay; execution costs; potentially material regulatory/control risk; monitoring/risk-management burden.

Important: repository evidence does not establish that AE currently has any exploitable market edge. This topology is strategically important because it was largely absent from prior commercialization framing, not because trading is already preferred.

### T4 — Monetize attention
AE turns resolved uncertainty into audience/traffic and captures advertising/sponsorship/platform revenue.

Economic chain:
ASYMMETRY → CONTENT/UTILITY → ATTENTION → ADVERTISER/PLATFORM PAYMENT

Strength:
low fulfillment/support and potentially reusable content machinery.

Weakness:
traffic scale and platform economics may dominate; weak revenue density; value capture may be only loosely coupled to resolution quality.

### T5 — Own the product/transaction
AE discovers the opportunity and owns the offered product/service transaction.

Examples:
- digital product;
- micro-SaaS;
- marketplace;
- commerce product;
- specialized workflow tool.

Economic chain:
ASYMMETRY → PRODUCT → CUSTOMER → MARGIN

Strength:
greater capture upside/control; direct customer and transaction telemetry.

Weakness:
product, acquisition, support, maintenance and operational burden; potentially furthest from low-maintenance first proof.

### T6 — License/sell the mechanism or asset
AE creates an information/resolution mechanism and licenses/sells it to an incumbent with complementary assets.

Economic chain:
ASYMMETRY → RESOLUTION/MECHANISM → LICENSE/TRANSFER → FEE/ROYALTY

Strength:
can outsource commercialization and operations.

Weakness:
enterprise access/sales, appropriability, negotiation and long sales cycles; poor fit with fast first-market-truth unless a ready counterparty exists.

## Search strategy is orthogonal to capture topology
Independently of T1–T6, AE can search through:
- novel/residual asymmetry discovery;
- commercially constrained high-intent problems;
- narrow vertical/domain;
- proven-market adaptation: begin with demonstrated transaction and find underserved variation;
- direct-exploitation opportunity space where executable discrepancies recur.

Therefore “affiliate versus trading” is not a single-axis comparison. It combines different capture topology, search space, risk model and validation method.

## Experimental regime is also orthogonal
Two broad regimes remain plausible:
- evidence-before-build/action;
- market/economic-action-as-experiment after cheap fatal gates.

The crossover depends on construction/action cost, downside, observability and reversibility. Direct exploitation may require stronger pre-action validation than a €20 disposable affiliate/content test because capital loss and statistical false discovery can be materially higher.

## Independent AE-fit assessment

### Strong structural fit: T2 referral/lead
Why:
- AE already reasons about consequential decisions and information gaps;
- fulfillment can sit outside AE;
- transaction streams may already exist;
- commercial telemetry can be clearer than public actor-response experiments.

Unknown:
- traffic acquisition;
- affiliate/lead program availability;
- commission/margin;
- attribution;
- maintenance;
- whether AE can outperform existing decision content.

### Strong but unproven structural fit: T3 direct exploitation
Why:
- removes buyer/WTP/access-to-actor layers;
- economic measurement can be direct;
- repeated market observations can support recurrence/automation;
- conceptually close to “asymmetry engine.”

Unknown / potentially adverse:
- whether recoverable public information produces durable edge after costs;
- statistical proof burden;
- capital/risk;
- competition;
- execution latency;
- legal/regulatory constraints;
- regime decay and monitoring burden.

This topology deserves explicit Phase II consideration and cannot be omitted from a strategy tournament.

### Strong fit for first transaction but weaker passive trajectory: T1 manual/paid information
Why:
- direct payment evidence;
- cheap construction;
- uses existing resolution capability.

Weakness:
- customer acquisition and fulfillment;
- recurrence may remain human-bound unless standardized.

### Medium fit: T5 micro-product/SaaS
Why:
- AI lowers construction cost;
- direct transaction.

Weakness:
- AE has no evidence that product building is the bottleneck;
- acquisition/support/maintenance remain;
- risks recreating “build before market truth.”

### Medium/weak for first proof: T4 attention
Potentially low operational burden but typically requires audience scale before meaningful capture.

### Weak for rapid first proof: T6 licensing
Potentially attractive long-term but access/sales-cycle distance conflicts with velocity objective.

## Blind ranking by objective, not user suggestion
No single topology can be honestly declared winner from pre-056 repository evidence.

The independent shortlist is:
1. **T2 referral/lead / third-party transaction capture** — strong low-fulfillment structural fit.
2. **T3 direct exploitation** — strongest intrinsic monetization/automation fit, but with the largest unresolved edge/risk/statistical questions.
3. **T1 standardized paid information/tool** — strongest simple direct-payment test, weaker low-maintenance characteristics unless standardized.
4. T5 own product.
5. T4 attention.
6. T6 licensing for first proof.

This is a shortlist, not a substrate decision.

## Affiliate contamination test — blind answer
Would affiliate/referral emerge near the top if the human had never suggested it?

**YES, as a topology family (T2), but NOT as an independently justified preferred implementation.**

Reason: low fulfillment/support, existing transactions and decision-resolution fit follow from AE's objective/capabilities. However, repository evidence alone does not establish affiliate program economics, traffic acquisition, attribution or niche availability. “Affiliate first” would be stronger than the evidence.

## Trading/direct-exploitation challenge — blind answer
Could direct exploitation dominate affiliate/referral?

**YES under specific assumptions, currently untested.**

It can dominate if:
- AE can repeatedly detect executable edges from legitimately available information;
- expected edge survives fees/slippage/latency and competition;
- statistical validation can distinguish signal from noise fast enough;
- bounded capital risk is acceptable;
- regulatory/control distance is manageable;
- ongoing monitoring does not violate the low-maintenance objective.

It loses if these assumptions fail. Pre-056 evidence supplies no direct test of them.

## Key contamination finding
The strategy space must be defined first at the value-capture-topology level. A tournament composed mainly of affiliate, lead-gen, paid information, micro-products and services is structurally biased toward “sell/mediate information to humans” and omits the economically distinct T3 direct-exploitation family.

## Substrate-independent findings likely to survive any strategy choice
Pre-056 evidence independently supports testing:
- economic outcomes earlier;
- market/economic truth over proxy evidence when cheaper and sufficiently observable;
- qualified exposure/action denominator before interpreting failure;
- strong limits on meta-work;
- existing transaction streams/proven markets as valuable priors;
- bounded real capital where it buys stronger evidence;
- recurrence and operator burden as first-class economics;
- automation after a mechanism/repeated mechanic is demonstrated;
- separating first economic proof from eventual low-maintenance/passive state.

These do not select affiliate, trading or another substrate by themselves.

## Frozen conclusion before reading 056
The correct independent-review question is not “is affiliate good?”

It is:
> Did Experiment 056 search a sufficiently complete value-capture topology space before preferring a substrate, and are its substrate-independent Phase II rules separable from any contaminated substrate recommendation?

The blind map is now frozen for comparison.
