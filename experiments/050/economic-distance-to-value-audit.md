## 14. Decision-actor versus buyer/payer finding

**[DERIVED]** AE has become materially better at finding identifiable decision actors, especially after Experiments 028 and 032. It has not demonstrated comparable ability to identify buyers, payers, procurement authority, or observable transaction counterparties. CRM and realtime actors control operational decisions but are not evidenced buyers of AE resolutions; Superset's decision participant has no payer topology; the Canadian importer was never acquired; cocoa named plausible direct customers but never obtained valid exposure. Therefore:

```text
DECISION ACTOR IDENTIFIED
≠
BUYER / PAYER IDENTIFIED
```

The distinction is the clearest current economic-distance gap.

## 21. Funnel / terminal-boundary finding

**[DERIVED]** Among the five material chains audited deeply:

- one reached a specified WTP instrument but failed before valid exposure (cocoa);
- one reached decision-ready resolution but failed actor/distribution access (Canadian);
- one reached public delivery but not exposure/effect (CRM);
- one reached actor/decision-state effect but no economic-effect or buyer layer (Superset);
- one remained pre-resolution with delivery status unknown (realtime).

This is not a conversion funnel and the cases are heterogeneous. The shared economic boundary is that no chain joins a validated resolution/effect with an identified payer and observable exchange path.

## 22. Earlier-economic-discriminator counterfactual

- **Cocoa:** **[DERIVED]** no earlier discriminator is supported; the paid manual pilot was already the economic discriminator, but the observation channel was invalid.
- **Canadian:** **[DERIVED]** a paid manual resolution could be a future hypothesis, but historically no reachable qualifying importer or baseline existed. WTP first would confound access, resolution effect, and payment.
- **CRM:** **[DERIVED]** adding pricing or solicitation to the one permitted public reply would have changed the treatment, violated its controls, and remained uninterpretable with UNKNOWN exposure.
- **Superset:** **[DERIVED]** no legitimate economic discriminator existed within the project-governance decision; payer discovery would be a separate opportunity.
- **Realtime:** **[DERIVED]** payment intent could not resolve the missing workload matrix or construct the resolution, and public delivery itself remained unknown.

**[DERIVED]** No historical chain supports the claim that a valid transaction test was plainly available and merely deferred. However, the combined pattern—manual resolutions can be cheap, actor topology can be explicit, and a paid manual pilot can be bounded—supports testing economic proximity during future selection rather than inserting WTP into immature chains.

## 23. Anti-premature-monetization challenge result

**PASS WITH MATERIAL LIMITS.** **[DERIVED]** Pushing payment earlier in the audited chains would often have produced ambiguous or invalid evidence because the resolution, actor access, exposure, decision effect, or payer role was unresolved. Stated WTP would be especially weak without an exchange action. A paid manual service could sometimes avoid software, but it adds commercial authorization, tax/accounting, platform, privacy, support, liability, and delivery obligations. Economic proximity must therefore operate only after fatal gates and resolution plausibility, never as a substitute for them.

## 24. Research-budget allocation finding

**[DERIVED]** By primary intent, 24/49 experiments focused on Engine/architecture or research policy, 17/49 on opportunity discovery/discrimination, 3/49 on resolution construction, 4/49 on actor/decision-effect testing, 1/49 on WTP/exchange intent, and 0/49 on economic effect or transaction/value capture. This is not a claim about equal effort per experiment. The allocation was historically rational: it created source/evidence discipline, fatal gates, exact-resolution checks, decision-ready FORGE outputs, authorization controls, and interaction semantics while preventing invalid commercial inference. It also means the economic frontier has received very little direct experimental budget.

## 25. Marginal-learning finding

**[RECORDED]** By Experiment 049 the repository had aligned its living documentation, implemented the one earned persistence change, replicated fatal-gate discovery, produced multiple manual resolutions, distinguished delivery/exposure/effect, and tested agentic/latency hypotheses. **[DERIVED]** Additional generic architecture or upstream policy audits are now less likely than buyer/exchange evidence to change the probability of first value capture. Upstream work remains valuable when it resolves a candidate-specific fatal uncertainty; it should not continue merely because it is easier to observe than economic exchange.

## 26. Time-to-value-capture telemetry finding

**[DERIVED]** `TIME TO DECISION EVIDENCE`, `TIME TO ECONOMIC EFFECT EVIDENCE`, and `TIME TO VALUE-CAPTURE EVIDENCE` must remain separate. Decision-evidence timing exists for some chains; no economic-effect or value-capture event exists, so historical TVC is right-censored and cannot be calculated. Future records should preserve admission time, frontier transitions, buyer/payer state, exchange attempt validity, and terminal/censor reason manually. No telemetry software is justified.

## 27. P0 current-policy assessment

**[DERIVED]** P0 has strong benefits: it produced cheap rejection, protected validity, avoided premature building, and enabled the sole material actor-effect result. Its failure mode is selection indifference among otherwise credible survivors that differ materially in buyer visibility and exchange distance. Continuing P0 unchanged risks optimizing what AE can readily learn rather than what can reach captured value, although the historical record does not show that P0 deliberately rejected a valid transaction-ready candidate.

## 28. P1 revenue-first assessment

**REJECT.** **[DERIVED]** P1 would have pushed immature Canadian, CRM, Superset, and realtime cases toward price or payment before actor access, resolution effect, or buyer identity was sound. It risks cheap-talk WTP, false negatives, trivial easy-to-charge opportunities, legal/tax/platform/support burden, contamination of natural decision experiments, and suppression of indirect or high-value infrastructure opportunities. Revenue is strong evidence when valid; earliest possible revenue is not a safe primary selector.

## 29. P2 evidence-valid economic-proximity assessment

**EARNED AS A HYPOTHESIS, NOT POLICY.** **[DERIVED]** P2 preserves P0's fatal gates and resolution validity, then asks whether otherwise credible opportunities differ in unresolved distance to an observable exchange/value-capture test. Its benefits are explicit buyer/payer scrutiny, earlier exposure of missing transaction topology, and focus on first value-capture proof without requiring software. Its risks are bias toward trivial direct-payment cases, duplication of economic consequence, penalties against indirect monetization, premature selling, cheap-talk WTP, and hidden compliance/support cost. Those risks require a bounded manual comparison, qualitative fields, and no additive score.
